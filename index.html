Phase 1 · Database 2 · EEG (XiangyueZhao/Supplement-Data)

The brain's answer gets louder and faster as pain rises.

The repository pools nine laser-pain EEG experiments (Biosemi, ANT and Brain Products systems). It holds every single trial's laser energy and 0–10 pain rating, plus Cz waveforms averaged per person for each rating level and each laser energy.

At Cz, the response peak to peak (N2 to P2) rises in a near-straight line with the rating: about per rating point, r² across ten levels. The N2 also arrives earlier, from about 253 ms at rating 1 to about 207 ms at rating 10. That gives a defensible, measured map from a pain number to a neural signature.

Four pain profiles across 678 people

Each dot is a person, placed by two summary axes of their own rating pattern. Click a dot to see that person.

Axis 1 ≈ louder, rising and stickier ratings. Axis 2 ≈ more trial-to-trial volatility.

Profiles come from four features of each person's rating series, each compared with others in the same experiment so that equipment and protocol differences don't create fake groups: level (how much it hurts), volatility (scatter around their own trend), drift (habituation or sensitisation over the session) and stickiness (whether one trial's surprise carries into the next). Every experiment contributes to every profile. Treat these as regions of a continuum rather than fixed types: cluster separation is modest (silhouette 0.20), and resampling agrees on the grouping about half the time beyond chance (bootstrap ARI 0.48).

    Phase 1 · Database 1 · Hawking retest verbatims

    People hear pain as constant, then as a pulse.

    600 adults aged 18–75, in four test cells of 150, answered one question: how would you describe your pain if it was a noise you can't escape? Each answer is coded for time pattern, timbre, pitch, loudness and body cues (one answer can carry several codes).

    Constant is the dominant idea (27%). The next cluster is rhythmic: hammering, drums, pounding, and the pulse words almost always come with an impact sound. Ringing, buzzing and static, the tinnitus family, appear in about one answer in nine. 113 answers (19%) give no sound at all ("not sure", or a reaction to the ad), so read rates as a share of everyone.

      Phase 2 · Pain-to-relief, fitted to two clinical trials

      A relief curve the sound can travel along.

      The transition isn't invented: it chains measured relationships, each replaceable by better data.

      Trial results → pain rating over time → neural response at that rating → sound → alpha silence

      Both trials test the same fixed combination of acetaminophen and naproxen sodium against placebo. They posted relief as time-weighted sums of pain-intensity difference (SPID) over time windows, not hour-by-hour scores. Each curve's shape therefore comes from known pharmacology (how fast each drug peaks in the blood and clears), and its height and onset are fitted so that the area under it matches each published SPID.

      What the fit can claim

      Each dental arm has two published windows and two fitted numbers (peak relief, onset delay), so its curve matches the published sums by construction. The shape between and within windows comes from pharmacology, not from the trial.

      Rescue medicine shapes the curves

      When a patient took rescue medicine, the trials froze their pain at the last score before it. The curves show relief as the trials counted it, with no credit for the rescue drug. In the bunion trial 81% of patients on the combination needed rescue within 12 hours, so its curve builds slowly and is the less certain of the two.

      Assumed, not published

      Average starting pain isn't in the posted results. Both trials required at least 5 of 10; 7 is assumed here and can be changed. Hour-by-hour pain scores would confirm or correct each curve's shape point by point.

      Method notes

      EEG. Single-trial CSVs from all nine experiments; ratings above 10 and laser energy 0 dropped. For each person: mean rating, residual SD after removing the effect of laser energy and a linear session trend, the trend slope, and lag-1 autocorrelation of residuals. Features z-scored within experiment, clipped at ±3, k-means with k = 4 (silhouette 0.18–0.20 across k = 2–7, best at 4). Gain (rating change per joule) is shown for the 518 people who received more than one energy.

      Waveforms. all_CZ_rating.mat and all_CZ_power.mat (Cz, −1000 to 1999 ms, 1 kHz). Peaks: N2 minimum at 120–300 ms, P2 maximum at 250–500 ms, on the group mean. Per-person averages are pooled per rating level and aren't linked across levels, so neural profiles per person aren't possible from this release. ERP-power.mat in the repository is truncated and couldn't be read.

      Verbatims. Keyword-coded with transparent rules (one answer can carry several codes). This is a first pass: before Phase 3, two people should code a sample by hand to check agreement. The answers were collected after respondents saw ad concepts, so some reflect the ad's own language.

      Relief model. Fitted to the posted results of NCT05761574 (dental, single dose) and NCT05868122 (bunion, every 12 h for 48 h). Blood level per drug uses typical published oral values (acetaminophen peak 0.75 h, half-life 2.5 h; naproxen sodium peak 1 h, half-life 14 h). An effect-site delay and an Emax response (Hill 1.5) turn blood level into relief; the combination joins both drugs as independent effects. For each arm, peak relief and onset delay are grid-fitted so the area under the curve matches each published SPID window. Bunion placebo is fitted as natural post-surgical recovery (a power curve), and the drug’s relief is added on top. Pain = start × (1 − PID ÷ 7). Thresholds mark 30% and 50% reductions, the IMMPACT benchmarks for moderate and substantial improvement. Alpha share = 1 − rating/10.
